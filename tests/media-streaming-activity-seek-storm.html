ManagedMediaSource seek storms

Case 1 is the shape the reporting site actually has, and the one to run first. It plays to the end, and from its ended listener it seeks to a duration it holds in its own database, about 1e-8 longer than the one we compute from the media. Step 6 of the seek algorithm clamps that away, so the seek starts from a position already in ended playback and transitions into nothing. Pre-fix WebKit still runs a full seek — HTMLMediaElement::seekTask's same-position short circuit is bypassed whenever a MediaSource is attached — which clears m_sentEndEvent and fires ended again, re-entering the site's listener. Unbounded, about 2500 iterations per second on the real page.

Case 2 is the same seek in isolation, driven from a paused element rather than from playback. It aims past duration for the same reason case 1 does: a seek to video.duration cannot reach end of media unless duration is representable at MediaTime::DefaultTimeScale, because the target arrives as a DoubleValue that std::min keeps on equality and computeSeekTime then rounds to 1/10000000. Case 3 is unrelated to end of media — it is the buffered/unbuffered churn that exercises the NetworkProcess notification rate limiter. Each startstreaming/endstreaming event reported below is one media-state transition delivered from WebCore to the UIProcess and on to the NetworkProcess, with no coalescing in between: ManagedMediaSource::setStreaming → HasStreamingActivity → WebProcessProxy::updateMediaStreamingActivity. See 319847@main (bug 322488, cab391584f80).
